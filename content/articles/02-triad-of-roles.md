# Triad of Roles: Coach, Scrum Master & Project/Delivery Manager

*Module 02 · Clarifying overlaps & borders · 6 min read*

## Key takeaways

- The **Scrum Master** is an accountability defined by Scrum, for **one Scrum Team** (Schwaber & Sutherland, 2020).
- The **Agile Coach** works with **any agile approach**, often across **several teams** or the whole organisation. Scrum does not define this role.
- The **Project/Delivery Manager** has **hierarchical or formal authority** and is responsible for results.
- In a crisis, the three roles act very differently: the manager **decides**, the Scrum Master **removes impediments**, and the coach **helps the team handle it and learn from it**.

---

## 1. Why these roles get confused

- A Scrum Master uses coaching skills every day. The Scrum Guide lists "Coaching the team members in self-management and cross-functionality" as one of the ways the Scrum Master serves the team (Schwaber & Sutherland, 2020).
- Many companies give experienced Scrum Masters the job title "Agile Coach", even when the work stays the same.
- All three roles care about delivery, so from the outside they can look alike. The difference is **how** each one acts and **what authority** it has.

## 2. The comparison matrix

| | **Agile Coach** | **Scrum Master** | **Project / Delivery Manager** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Scope** | One or several teams, sometimes the organisation | One Scrum Team, and helping the wider organisation adopt Scrum | A team, a department or a project |
| **Framework** | Any agile approach | Scrum | Not tied to a framework |
| **Authority style** | None over people: influences and supports | None over people: serves the team ("true leaders who serve") | Hierarchical or formal authority |
| **Main goal** | Teams that improve on their own | Scrum working well, an effective Scrum Team | Results and people management |
| **Crisis handling** | Helps the team handle the crisis itself; afterwards, helps it learn and works with leaders on causes outside the team | Causes the removal of impediments; keeps the events focused on the goal | Decides on priorities, people and resources; reports to stakeholders |

*Sources: research document, section 3 (Adkins, 2010; Schwaber & Sutherland, 2020). The "Crisis handling" row is a summary written by the project team from these sources; see section 4.*

> **Note on the "manager" column.** The research document compares the coach with a **manager** in general. A project or delivery manager is one kind of manager: the role with formal responsibility for delivering a project. Scrum itself has no project manager: the Scrum Guide defines only three accountabilities, "the Developers, the Product Owner, and the Scrum Master" (Schwaber & Sutherland, 2020).

## 3. One sentence per role

- **Scrum Master:** "accountable for establishing Scrum as defined in the Scrum Guide" and "for the Scrum Team's effectiveness" (Schwaber & Sutherland, 2020).
- **Agile Coach:** helps teams and organisations work in an agile way and keep improving on their own, without authority over people (Adkins, 2010).
- **Project/Delivery Manager:** responsible for results through formal authority. In an agile organisation, Appelo argues that managers should "manage the system, not the people" (Appelo, 2011).

## 4. Crisis handling: who owns what?

**The crisis.** Two days before an important release, the team discovers a serious bug. Stakeholders are worried and the team is stressed. *(Scenario written by the project team.)*

| Role | What this role does | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Project / Delivery Manager** | Decides whether to delay the release or reduce scope, assigns extra people if needed, and informs stakeholders. | The manager has formal authority over results and resources (research document, section 3). |
| **Scrum Master** | Removes impediments that block the fix (for example, access to a test environment), protects the team from interruptions, and keeps the Daily Scrum short and focused. | The Scrum Guide asks the Scrum Master to cause "the removal of impediments to the Scrum Team's progress" (Schwaber & Sutherland, 2020). |
| **Agile Coach** | Does not take over. Helps the team stay calm and decide its own plan, for example by facilitating a short session ("What options do we have?"). After the crisis, helps the team learn from it in a retrospective, and works with leaders if the cause is outside the team. | The coach has no authority and aims for teams that improve on their own (Adkins, 2010). |

**The key difference:** in a crisis, the manager acts **for** the team, the Scrum Master acts **around** the team (clearing the way), and the coach acts **with** the team (helping it decide and learn). *(Summary written by the project team.)*

## 5. More situations

| Situation | Good coaching response | Stance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Product Owner tells developers how to work | One-to-one talk, start from the PO's concerns, explain self-management, propose a one-sprint experiment | Mentor, coach |
| A manager wants to rank teams by velocity | Ask what the manager needs to know; explain why velocity is not comparable between teams; propose outcome or flow metrics | Teacher, coach |
| The Daily Scrum lasts 40 minutes | Make it visible; let the team redesign it in the retrospective; recall the 15-minute timebox | Facilitator, teacher |

*Source: research document, section 9 (Cohn, 2005; Schwaber & Sutherland, 2020).*

## Common misconceptions

| Misconception | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| "Agile Coach and Scrum Master are the same thing." | No. They differ in scope (several teams vs one Scrum Team) and origin (coaching practice vs the Scrum Guide). |
| "The Scrum Master is the team's project manager." | No. Scrum has no project manager. Scrum Teams are self-managing, and the Scrum Master serves them. |
| "The coach takes charge when things go wrong." | No. The coach helps the team handle the problem and learn from it. Decisions on resources belong to the manager. |
