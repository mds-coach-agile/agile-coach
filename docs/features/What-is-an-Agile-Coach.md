# What is an Agile Coach?

An **Agile Coach** helps teams and organisations learn to work in an agile way, and then to keep improving **on their own**. The coach works alongside the team, not above it. They don't manage people, assign work or do the team's job (Adkins, 2010; Davies & Sedley, 2009).

ICAgile, one of the main agile certification bodies, describes the role like this: "An agile team coach guides individuals and teams to reach high performance." The coach does this by combining "skills from professional facilitation, professional coaching, mentoring, and teaching" (ICAgile, n.d.).

## Core responsibilities

| Responsibility | What it looks like in practice |
|---|---|
| **Teaching** | Explains agile principles and practices (Scrum, Kanban, XP) when the team doesn't know them yet. |
| **Facilitating** | Runs events and workshops so the group reaches its *own* decisions, and stays neutral on the content. |
| **Mentoring** | Supports Scrum Masters, Product Owners and managers who are learning their role. |
| **Coaching** | Asks open questions ("What options do you see?") so people find their own answers. |
| **Observing and giving feedback** | Watches how the team works and makes patterns visible to the team. |
| **Working with leaders** | Helps managers and leaders remove obstacles that are outside the team's control. |

These activities match the four coaching **stances** in the Agile Coaching Competency Framework: teacher, mentor, facilitator and professional coach. The coach chooses the stance that fits the situation (Spayd & Adkins, 2011).

## Focus: team autonomy

Everything an Agile Coach does points to one goal: **a self-managing team that improves by itself**.

- The Scrum Guide describes Scrum Teams as "self-managing, meaning they internally decide who does what, when, and how" (Schwaber & Sutherland, 2020, "Scrum Team" section).
- The coach helps the team *grow into* that self-management instead of deciding for it, and relies on the team to choose its own path (Adkins, 2010).
- The coach gradually steps back. They teach more when a team is new, and ask more questions and give less direction as the team matures. Success means the team can keep improving without the coach (Adkins, 2010; Spayd & Adkins, 2011).

**In short:** a manager is responsible for getting results through the team. A coach is responsible for helping the team become able to get results by itself. *(Summary written by the project team.)*

## Agile Coach vs Scrum Master vs Manager

| | **Agile Coach** | **Scrum Master** | **Manager** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | One or several teams, sometimes the whole organisation | One Scrum Team, plus helping the wider organisation adopt Scrum | The team or department they're formally responsible for |
| **Frameworks** | Any agile approach (Scrum, Kanban, XP, scaled frameworks) | Scrum, as defined in the Scrum Guide | Not tied to a framework |
| **Authority** | No authority over people: works through influence, questions and support | No authority over people: leads by serving the team | Formal authority in the organisation's hierarchy |
| **Main goal** | Teams and organisations that improve on their own | Scrum understood and working well, and an effective Scrum Team | Business results, and a working environment (the "system") in which people can perform and grow |

*Sources: Adkins (2010); Schwaber & Sutherland (2020); Appelo (2011); ICAgile (n.d.).*

### How to tell them apart

- **Scrum Master:** an *accountability inside Scrum*. The Scrum Guide says: "The Scrum Master is accountable for establishing Scrum as defined in the Scrum Guide" and "for the Scrum Team's effectiveness". It also says: "Scrum Masters are true leaders who serve the Scrum Team and the larger organization" (Schwaber & Sutherland, 2020, "Scrum Master" section).
- **Agile Coach:** a role that comes from *industry practice and coaching frameworks*, not from Scrum. It isn't limited to one framework or one team, and it has its own certification tracks, for example ICAgile's Agile Team Coaching track (ICP-ACC, ICE-AC) (ICAgile, n.d.; Spayd & Adkins, 2011).
- **Manager:** a role in the *organisation's hierarchy*. Appelo argues that in an agile organisation, managers should "manage the system, not the people". They shape the environment and the constraints so teams can self-organise, instead of directing daily work (Appelo, 2011).

### Why people confuse them

- A Scrum Master **uses coaching skills** every day. The Scrum Guide lists "Coaching the team members in self-management and cross-functionality" as one of the ways the Scrum Master serves the team (Schwaber & Sutherland, 2020, "Scrum Master" section).
- Many companies give experienced Scrum Masters the job title "Agile Coach", even when the work is the same.
- Both the coach and the Scrum Master lead without formal authority, which is unusual compared with a manager.

### Common misconceptions

| Misconception | Reality |
|---|---|
| "The Agile Coach is the team's boss." | No. The coach has no hierarchical authority over the team. |
| "Agile Coach and Scrum Master are the same thing." | No. They differ in scope (several teams vs one Scrum Team) and origin (coaching practice vs the Scrum Guide). |
| "The Scrum Master manages the team." | No. Scrum Teams are self-managing, and the Scrum Master serves them. |
| "A coach always gives the answers." | Only when teaching or mentoring. In the coaching stance, the coach asks questions so the team finds the answer. |

## Does the Scrum Guide define an "Agile Coach"?

> ⚠️ **No.** *The Scrum Guide 2020* does **not** formally define an "Agile Coach" role.

The Scrum Guide 2020 says: "Scrum defines three specific accountabilities within the Scrum Team: the Developers, the Product Owner, and the Scrum Master" (Schwaber & Sutherland, 2020, "Scrum Team" section).

The terms "Agile Coach" and "manager" don't appear anywhere in the Guide. The Guide does describe *coaching* as something the **Scrum Master** does, but there it's an activity, not a separate role.

The Agile Coach role comes from **industry practice** and from coaching frameworks, such as:
- the Agile Coaching Institute's competency framework (Spayd & Adkins, 2011)
- Adkins' book *Coaching Agile Teams* (2010)
- ICAgile's Agile Team Coaching track (ICAgile, n.d.)

**What this means for you:** if a question asks about "Scrum roles" or "Scrum accountabilities", the Agile Coach is **not** one of them.
